# Critique lenses

Use this as an evidence checklist, not a requirement to mention every category. Prefer the clearest visible tensions and name them with the most precise architectural concept available.

## Universal scan

### Parti, massing, and silhouette

- No legible primary/secondary/tertiary hierarchy
- Competing volumes that refuse a coherent parti
- Additions that read as separate buildings in an unwilling group project
- Abrupt setbacks, cantilevers, bays, or appendages without a regulating logic
- A top, middle, and base that appear to belong to different concepts
- Residual or accidental poche masquerading as spatial depth
- Scale changes that make an entrance or occupied floor feel toy-sized

### Datum, grid, and proportion

- Windows, cornices, sills, stringcourses, or material joints that almost establish a datum and then abandon it
- Structural bays and facade bays visibly disagreeing
- Symmetry that fails at the last possible moment
- Proportional systems that change between adjacent volumes
- Repetition without rhythm, or rhythm interrupted without emphasis
- Intentional asymmetry that is balanced: do not call it a flaw merely because it is not mirrored

### Roof and crown

- Gable multiplication, roof pitches that almost agree, trapped valleys, or tiny decorative roofs
- Flat-roof islands between pitched roofs
- Oversized dormers, undersized dormers, fake dormers, and vents pretending to be windows
- Tower crowns, fins, hats, and mechanical disguises that do not resolve the shaft below
- Parapets or cornices that stop, jog, or change their mind
- Roof forms that create a gratuitous section problem for no spatial payoff

### Openings, bays, and facade rhythm

- Windows that nearly align but miss
- Uneven spacing, inconsistent head heights, arbitrary size changes, or a lost grid
- Too many window species on one elevation
- Punched openings fighting curtain wall logic
- Blank walls beside over-windowed zones
- Entrances hidden by garages, columns, landscaping, arches, or competing doors
- Mullion patterns producing accidental spandrel comedy

### Materials, envelope, and tectonics

- Veneer that starts or stops without a believable edge or return
- Material changes unrelated to massing, structure, weathering, or hierarchy
- Historic details applied as thin costume or literal facadism
- Overscaled columns, underscaled shutters, false lintels, brackets with nothing to support
- Rainscreen panelization that creates more joints than architecture
- Rustication, quoins, reveals, or shadow gaps used as graphic decals
- Joints, trim, railings, scuppers, or downspouts accidentally becoming the main composition
- One expensive material asked to rescue several unresolved decisions
- Value-engineered substitutions that visibly preserve the rendering but lose the tectonic logic

### Function made visible

- Driveways, ramps, loading, parking, or service entries overwhelming the public face
- Doors that seem ceremonial while another door does the actual work
- Circulation routes that disappear, collide, or make a pedestrian negotiate car space
- Balconies with implausible privacy, depth, access, or usefulness
- Core, egress, mechanical, or servicing demands visibly distorting the facade
- Shade, weather protection, drainage, or maintenance problems only when visibly supported; phrase uncertain consequences as appearances, not facts

### Site, context, and urbanism

- Building-to-ground connection: plinth, moat, berm, podium, exposed foundation, or awkward grade change
- Landscaping used to conceal rather than compose
- Street walls interrupted by setbacks, curb cuts, blank podiums, or parking
- A scale, frontage, or material language that ignores its immediate neighbors
- Public realm left as residual space
- Form that follows pro forma so literally the spreadsheet is visible from the sidewalk
- Contextualism reduced to one copied cornice line

## Scene-specific prompts

### Houses and suburban facades

Check garage dominance, roof gymnastics, entry legibility, window alignment, shutter scale, veneer termination, chimney proportion, foundation exposure, driveway area, porch usefulness, and whether the rear elevation appears to have wandered to the front.

Useful professional pressure points include McMansion roof salad, lawyer foyers, remnant poche, Palladian quotations with no proportional discipline, clipped eaves, fake structural brackets, and a plan clearly developed by pushing rooms outward until the elevation surrendered.

### Skyscrapers and towers

Check podium-to-tower relationship, repetitive curtain wall, corner resolution, crown logic, mechanical-floor disguise, facade depth, blank street frontage, tower proportions, and whether the skyline gesture has any relationship to the occupied floors below.

Useful pressure points include the “decorated shed wearing a diagrid,” value-engineered fins, frit asked to perform urbanism, Miesian discipline losing a fight with the lease span, and crowns designed entirely for the broker’s aerial rendering.

### Skylines

Check silhouette variety, accidental gaps, competing crowns, sameness, abrupt height transitions, foreground infrastructure, waterfront edge, and whether individual icons form a coherent whole. Separate actual massing from telephoto compression and haze.

### Streetscapes

Check frontage continuity, door frequency, blank walls, curb cuts, shade, pedestrian scale, signage clutter, ground-floor transparency, utility conflicts, and transitions between old and new fabric. Notice where a contextual datum becomes facadism or where a podium treats pedestrians as a rendering entourage.

### Suburban sprawl and aerial views

Check road hierarchy, parking oceans, cul-de-sac repetition, sidewalk continuity, shade, block size, land left over between uses, stormwater landscapes, roof sameness, and destinations separated by barriers. Aim jokes at the planning pattern, not residents or lifestyles.

Professional references may include figure-ground, transects, desire lines, induced demand, setbacks as land-use diagram, residual landscape, stormwater as amenity cosplay, and the sacred geometry of the fire-truck turning radius.

## Evidence language

Use certainty proportional to what the image proves:

- Directly visible: “the third-floor windows abandon the datum.”
- Visual interpretation: “the entry reads like a service door with a publicist.”
- Uncertain consequence: “this roof valley looks ready to convene a water-feature jury.”
- Prohibited leap: “this roof leaks,” “this violates code,” “this is structurally unsafe,” or “the architect was incompetent.”

Do not identify an architectural style, era, author, or location unless the evidence is clear or the user supplied it. A wrong attribution ruins a good joke.
